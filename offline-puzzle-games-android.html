Before you go offline

Install the game while connected, then open it and check that the rounds you want to play are available on your device. Locally available rounds can be played offline, with progress saved on the device. Additional downloads require an internet connection.

Online rankings, cloud features, purchases and optional rewarded ads also need connectivity and service availability. If you are preparing for a journey, test the game with your connection switched off before leaving. That tells you exactly what is ready to play on your phone.

Start by finding the target that is hardest to reach

In a chain reaction puzzle game, the largest group of particles often catches your attention first. Before aiming there, look at the edges and the bottom of the arena. An isolated target may need a separate spark or a connection that is available only briefly.

Consider a field with most targets near the top and two moving particles near the bottom. Clearing the upper cluster looks impressive, but the round may still be unfinished. Watch whether an Entangled partner can carry the reaction across the gap, or whether the lower particles will move within reach of another explosion. If there is no useful connection, plan to keep a tap for them.

Three decisions to make before a spark

  1. Where will the first reaction begin? Choose a particle or group that gives the chain somewhere useful to go.
  2. What will still be moving when it reaches the next group? Aim for the position a target is approaching, while allowing the spark time to grow.
  3. Which survivor will need another tap? Keep track of isolated targets while the first chain runs.

The in-game remaining tap allowance is your limit. An accepted tap that misses still spends a charge, so repeated tapping can leave you without a way to finish. You can place a second spark while a chain is active; use that opportunity when it improves the plan.

A moving Black Hole changes a safe-looking route

A Black Hole is a hazard rather than an ordinary target. Its movement matters: a spark placed far away can start a chain that later reaches it. Look beyond the first explosion and follow the likely route through nearby particles.

When a target passes close to a Black Hole, waiting may create separation. It can also close a gap elsewhere, so watch both the target and the hazard. Check the hole’s displayed protection state before acting. Different particle interactions can change the outcome; the particle guide explains the individual rules.

Turn a failed level into a useful clue

A retry gives you information. First identify what ended the attempt: a missed target, an exhausted tap allowance, the objective sequence or a hazard. Then change one part of your approach.

  • One target survived: start from the opposite side, wait for a better connection or reserve a spark for that survivor.
  • The chain reached a hazard: choose a different starting group or wait for more separation.
  • A spark missed: adjust its timing or position rather than repeating the same tap.
  • The objective was incomplete: check whether the round requires a specific arrow sequence as well as clearing targets.

This makes level-based play rewarding: you can compare attempts and understand which decision helped. Once you can finish a round, revisit it to try a different chain and improve your stars or score.

Choosing the best physics puzzle games for Android for your play style

If you enjoy predicting movement and learning interactions, look for a puzzle that makes the result of each move understandable. If you prefer short sessions, check whether progress is saved locally and whether you can return to earlier levels. For offline play, check the features you actually use rather than relying on a broad “offline” label.
