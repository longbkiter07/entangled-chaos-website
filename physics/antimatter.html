THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE GAME

Antimatter: the partner matter did not quite erase

Paul Dirac’s work in 1928 helped lead to the idea of an anti-electron; the positron was discovered in 1932. What began as a surprising implication of an equation became a real object that experiments could study.

What happens when matter meets antimatter?

Antiparticles match their partners in mass while carrying opposite electric charge where applicable. Particle–antiparticle annihilation converts the initial system into other particles, such as photons. The excess of matter over antimatter in the observable universe remains an important research question. CERN: Antimatter ↗

The mystery after the fireworks

The most interesting real question is not just how annihilation releases energy. It is why enough matter survived for stars, planets and us to exist. A short puzzle interaction can be the beginning of that much larger story.
