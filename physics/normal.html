THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE GAME

Matter: the extraordinary inside the ordinary

Look at a stone, a glass of water or your own hand. They seem continuous, but the story changes as you zoom inward. Atoms contain electrons and nuclei; the protons and neutrons in those nuclei contain quarks. “Normal” is our everyday perspective, not the name of one fundamental particle.

What is ordinary matter made of?

Physicists organise the elementary building blocks into quarks and leptons. Electrons are leptons; quarks form composite particles such as protons. The Standard Model also describes force carriers. It is a tested framework, but it does not include gravity and is not a complete account of nature. CERN: The Standard Model ↗

Where the game takes a different path

The Normal particle is an invented, readable starting point for a puzzle. Real atoms are not little bombs waiting for a nearby screen tap, and ordinary particle encounters do not automatically create expanding explosions.

Why start here?

A familiar-looking body gives you a baseline. Once you understand an ordinary chain, the stranger abilities become easier to compare. In physics, too, a simple model is useful when you remember where its assumptions stop.
