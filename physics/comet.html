THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE GAME

Comets: frozen messengers from the early Solar System

Far from the Sun, a comet can be a quiet mixture of ice, dust and rocky material. As its orbit carries it inward, sunlight warms its surface. Gas and dust escape, creating the luminous surroundings that make a comet seem much larger than its solid nucleus.

Why do comets grow tails?

Comets preserve material from the Solar System’s formation. Their gas and dust form a coma and tails; solar radiation and the solar wind shape those tails. A tail is not simply exhaust pointing backward along a flight path, and a comet need not be on a collision course to appear spectacular. NASA: Comet facts ↗

Where the game takes a different path

The game’s regularly arriving Comet and chain-igniting tail are fictional. Real comets follow orbits rather than spawning from arena corners, and their tails do not ignite every object they touch.

Watch the path, not just the glow

A moving visitor changes a puzzle’s timing. That visual idea echoes a real observing habit: tracking where an object is going can tell you more than judging the brightest part of an image.
