THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE GAME

The Higgs field: a discovery about particle mass

An idea proposed in 1964 became a discovery at CERN in 2012. The Higgs boson gave scientists evidence for a field woven into our description of nature. The story is about how elementary particles acquire mass, not a substance that freezes the universe.

What did the Higgs boson reveal?

The Higgs boson is an excitation of the Higgs field. Interactions with that field give mass to elementary particles such as electrons and quarks. Photons remain massless. This does not mean that all the mass of everyday objects comes directly from the Higgs field: composite matter also involves the energy of its constituents and interactions. CERN: The Higgs boson ↗

Where the game takes a different path

The expanding freeze wave, timed detonation and fused Higgs are fictional. Real Higgs bosons do not stop nearby particles for three seconds, and a particle’s mass is not the same thing as friction or a brake on motion.

An invitation to ask a deeper question

The slowing-and-freezing image gives a difficult concept a game identity. The real question is subtler: why do different elementary particles have the masses they do, and how do experiments test the field behind that explanation?

Higgs in Entangled Chaos

Releases one gradually expanding freeze wave, with 2.2 times the radius of a Normal explosion and no explosion area. Frozen targets stop moving and explode after three seconds, or sooner when another blast reaches them. Higgs ignores other Higgs waves and Neutrino explosions. Two touching Higgs fuse into one larger body with four times the ordinary Higgs wave radius; the freeze still lasts three seconds. Photons and Neutrinos are immune. Higgs cannot absorb Photons; they pass through it. An unshielded Black Hole absorbs the whole wave without freezing.
