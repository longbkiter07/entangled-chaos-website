THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE GAME

Pulsars: the lighthouses made from stellar remnants

In 1967, Jocelyn Bell Burnell noticed a remarkably regular radio signal. The source was not a human transmitter but a rapidly rotating stellar remnant. The timing opened a new way to recognise objects that are far too distant to visit.

Why does a pulsar seem to flash?

A pulsar is a rotating neutron star whose radiation beam sweeps across our line of sight. The lighthouse comparison explains the pulse: the source can keep emitting while rotation brings its beam toward and away from us. The flashes are not a star repeatedly exploding and rebuilding itself. NASA: Pulsars, cosmic lighthouses ↗

Where the game takes a different path

The small blast and delayed echo in Entangled Chaos are a timing mechanic. They borrow the idea of a repeating signal, not the actual emission process, size or rotation of a neutron star.

Predict the next moment

A delayed effect rewards looking ahead. In the game you watch where a target will move; in astronomy, repeated pulses let observers study an otherwise remote object through a changing signal.
