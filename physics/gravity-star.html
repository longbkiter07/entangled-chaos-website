THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE GAME

Gravity and stars: how a cloud becomes a sun

A star begins long before it shines. In a cold cloud of gas and dust, a denser region can gather more material. Gravity draws the material inward; as the region contracts and heats, the story moves toward a protostar and eventually a shining star.

How does gravity help a star form?

NASA describes stars forming in molecular clouds, where growing concentrations of matter can collapse under gravity. The object changes throughout its life, depending on properties such as mass. Gravity is part of that story, not a command that makes every nearby body follow the same spiral. NASA: The life of stars ↗

Where the game takes a different path

Gravity Star and Super Gravity are game names. Their fixed influence range, contact fusion and special Black Hole interaction are authored abilities. In particular, a pair merging into a stronger game body is not a lesson in nuclear fusion inside a real star.

Two scales of “fusion”

A puzzle uses merging as an easy-to-read change of state. Stellar physics uses nuclear fusion to describe changes to atomic nuclei. Similar words can hide very different processes, so the game and the science deserve separate explanations.

Gravity Star in Entangled Chaos

Draws nearby bodies into a spiral. Gravity Stars pull especially strongly on each other and fuse on contact into Super Gravity, with a larger body, range and effect. Near an unshielded Black Hole they orbit rather than being absorbed; they do not repair its protection.
