THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE GAME

Magnetars: stellar remnants with extreme magnetic fields

After a massive star dies, its compact remnant may retain an exceptionally powerful magnetic field. A magnetar takes that possibility to an extreme. It is an astronomical object with a star’s history, not an elementary particle with a magnetic label.

What makes a magnetar extraordinary?

Magnetars are neutron stars with unusually intense magnetic fields. NASA describes how stressed fields can fracture the crust in starquakes and produce bursts of high-energy radiation. These objects let astronomers explore conditions far beyond ordinary laboratory magnets. NASA: Neutron stars are weird ↗

Where the game takes a different path

The game’s separate push/pull timers, captured bodies and doubled attraction or repulsion are inventions. Real magnetars do not switch the entire surrounding universe between two force modes on a two-second cycle; their fields are not an all-purpose pull on every material.

An invisible field made readable

The arena translates an unseen influence into motion and timed phases. That makes a useful strategy problem, while the real object’s story is about the life of a stellar remnant and the behaviour of matter in an extreme environment.

Magnetar in Entangled Chaos

Switches between push and pull every two seconds. Each Magnetar has its own timer; two or more have staggered phases. Opposite-phase Magnetars attract each other at double strength; matching phases repel at double strength. During pull, captured particles travel with it until the next push releases them. Neutrinos and Photons ignore the field. Near a Black Hole, the Magnetar moves inward during pull and outward during push. The Black Hole keeps its direction and cannot be captured.
